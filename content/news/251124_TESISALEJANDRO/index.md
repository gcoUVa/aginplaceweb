On 24 October 2025, Alejandro García Requejo, researcher of the AgeInPlace-UAH subproject, successfully defended his PhD thesis entitled 'Design of Ultrasound Device-Free Systems for Indoor Human Positioning' at the Universidad de Alcalá. The thesis is closely aligned with Task 4 of the project, focused on passive and non-intrusive monitoring technologies, and investigates novel device-free sensing systems capable of monitoring location without requiring users to wear or interact with any electronic device.
The research presents innovative ultrasonic sensing solutions that enable unobtrusive indoor monitoring through the analysis of wave reflections and occlusions caused by the human body. It includes the development of high-accuracy device-free localization systems, advanced signal processing techniques, real-time FPGA-based implementations, and simulation tools for evaluating indoor human monitoring scenarios. The proposed approaches achieved centimetre-level positioning accuracy in indoor environments, demonstrating their potential for supporting independent living and the early detection of behavioural changes in older adults. This work contributes directly to the objectives of AgeInPlace-UAH by advancing passive, privacy-preserving technologies for healthy ageing at home.  
The quality and impact of this research were further recognized when the thesis was awarded the First Prize for the Best PhD Thesis of 2025 by the Spanish Chapter of the IEEE Instrumentation and Measurement Society.
